CS184 · Fall 2026 Final Project Proposal

DrapeFit: Clothing on Adjustable Bodies

We propose an interactive 3D virtual try-on prototype that lets users adjust a body's proportions and compare how different garment sizes drape on it. We will combine editable body geometry with a mass–spring cloth simulation, body collisions, and a visualization of relative fabric stretch. Our goal is to explore approximate size and shape differences through graphics, while evaluating the limitations of our simulation.

Problem

Clothing photos show garments on particular bodies, while size labels do not show how one might hang on different proportions. We want users to explore drape and silhouette by adjusting a body and selecting garment sizes. This will be an approximate fit visualization, not a faithful digital twin, universal retailer system, or accurate size recommendation. Simulated stretch will not represent measured pressure.

We plan to parameterize a body triangle mesh with controls for height, chest, waist, hips, and shoulder width. Height and weight may provide a coarse preset, but cannot reconstruct an individual; users will correct proportions with measurements or sliders. We will update its collision representation as the mesh changes. Front and back garment panels will start around the body, connect at seams, and settle under gravity. Approximate compound body colliders will support the baseline. After a measurement edit, we will reset and resimulate the cloth.

Editable geometry, garment construction, numerical stability, seams, and intersection prevention are the central challenges. We will begin with one standing pose, one sleeveless top, three sizes, and one uncalibrated fabric model to study behavior and diagnose failures.

Goals

Baseline: what we plan to deliver

We plan to build one adjustable body with reproducible presets and one sleeveless top with sewn front and back panels. Three garment sizes will have dimensions independent of the body: changing body proportions will not resize the garment. A mass–spring solver will include gravity, damping, seams, body collisions, and basic self-collision.

A desktop demo will offer body controls, size selection, an orbit camera, reset and resimulate, and shaded and stretch-color views. The color map will show relative stretch: for each edge, max(0, (current length − rest length) / rest length), averaged at vertices. It will not be a measured pressure or validated comfort score. If a garment is too small for the chosen constraints, the demo will expose remaining stretch, intersections, or failure to settle instead of silently enlarging it.

Outputs will include side-by-side images for three body shapes by three garment sizes, a short video, and an implementation and results report.

Our contribution beyond HW4

HW4 will be a conceptual starting point for cloth behavior, not the full project. Our planned additions are locally adjustable body geometry, garment panel topology and seams, dimensioned sizes, a matching compound collision representation, and a controlled comparison and diagnostic workflow.

Questions and evaluation

  1. How will body proportions and garment dimensions affect silhouette and stretch patterns?
  2. How will mesh resolution, timestep, and spring stiffness affect runtime and stability?
  3. Where will a simplified body collision proxy produce visible errors?

We will run the 3-by-3 comparison with fixed fabric settings, pose, camera, and a documented settling rule or time budget. We will record cloth vertex count and test hardware, and report simulation milliseconds per step, total settling time, and rendering frame rate separately. We will compare at least two mesh resolutions and state their settings. Remaining cloth vertices inside the collision proxy, maximum penetration depth, and relative stretch summaries will diagnose the solver and proxy; they will not establish real-world fit accuracy.

Images will compare folds, silhouette, clipping, and failures. We will aim for responsive interaction. If garment measurements or photos become available, we may compare them qualitatively; stability alone will not validate clothing accuracy.

Stretch goals: what we hope to deliver

  1. More faithful cloth–body mesh collisions, with acceleration if needed.
  2. A second fabric parameter set and a discussion of its effects.
  3. Sleeves or a second garment type.
  4. Simple pose changes.

GPU acceleration, photorealistic rendering, body scanning, and ML training are outside the baseline.

Resources

We propose C++ with an OpenGL/GLSL viewer, drawing on course cloth concepts. The simulator will be a desktop program; this site is plain HTML and CSS. We expect to use team laptops and standard graphics hardware; the benchmark machine is TBD. Blender may help author geometry, but its cloth solver will not substitute for our implementation. We will prefer procedural or self-authored assets.

We will document the code starting point before development and distinguish permitted use of our HW4 work from new contributions. We will not copy or publish homework starter or solution code here; the course prohibits public posting of homework code.
