CS184 · Fall 2026 Final Project Proposal

myclos.et: A Playable Virtual Closet

myclos.et is an interactive 3D virtual try-on demo. Users shape a body with sliders, browse a small store of garments simulated on that body, and open any item in a live try-on view where the cloth drapes, collides, and blows in the wind in real time. Under the hood, we combine editable body geometry with a mass–spring cloth simulation, body and self-collisions, and a relative-stretch heatmap that shows where clothes are tight or loose.

Problem

Clothing photos show garments on particular bodies, and size labels do not show how a garment will hang on different proportions. We want users to explore drape, silhouette, and tightness by shaping a body, browsing a store of clothes on that body, and comparing sizes side by side. myclos.et is an approximate visualization, not a validated size-recommendation system.

We will parameterize a body triangle mesh with controls for height, chest, waist, hips, and shoulder width, plus presets, and update its collision representation as the mesh changes. Garments are built from front and back panels that start around the body, connect at seams, and settle under gravity. When the user drags a slider, the cloth keeps simulating and re-settles onto the new body live, instead of resetting.

The central challenges are editable body geometry, garment construction from panels and seams, numerical stability, and preventing cloth from passing through the body or itself, especially while the body is changing shape. Simulating a whole store of items on one body adds a performance and caching challenge.

Goals

Baseline: what we plan to deliver

  1. Adjustable body: live sliders for height, chest, waist, hips, and shoulder width, plus body presets.
  2. Garments: three garment templates (tank top, skirt, sleeveless dress), each in S/M/L. Garment dimensions are independent of the body, so changing proportions does not resize the garment.
  3. Cloth simulation: a mass–spring solver with seams, gravity, damping, body collisions, and self-collision.
  4. Wind: a wind force on the cloth with on/off, strength, and direction controls, scaled by how directly each triangle faces the wind, with light random gusting.
  5. Playable demo: a live try-on view with an orbit camera; a wardrobe panel and S/M/L size buttons; toggles for the stretch heatmap, wireframe, and turntable spin; all in a Dear ImGui interface.
  6. Store mode: about 10 items generated from template variations (length, fit, color/print). After the user sets their body once, each item is simulated on it and cached, and a store grid shows a thumbnail of the body wearing each item. Clicking an item opens it in the live try-on view.

The heatmap shows relative stretch: for each edge, max(0, (current length − rest length) / rest length), averaged at vertices. If a garment is too small for the body, the demo shows the remaining stretch or intersections instead of silently enlarging it.

Outputs will include side-by-side images for three body shapes by three garment sizes, a screenshot of the store grid, a short demo video, and an implementation and results report.

Our contribution beyond HW4

HW4 is a conceptual starting point for cloth behavior, not the full project. Our additions are locally adjustable body geometry, garment panel topology and seams, parameterized garment templates with dimensioned sizes, a body collision representation that updates live, wind, a cached store-mode pipeline, and an interactive try-on interface.

Questions and evaluation

  1. How do body proportions and garment dimensions affect silhouette and stretch patterns?
  2. How do mesh resolution, timestep, and spring stiffness affect runtime and stability?
  3. Can the simulation stay stable and interactive while the body changes shape live?
  4. Where does a simplified body collision proxy produce visible errors?

We will run the 3-by-3 comparison with fixed fabric settings, pose, camera, and a documented settling rule. We will report cloth vertex count, test hardware, simulation milliseconds per step, settling time, rendering frame rate, and the time to build the store grid. We will compare at least two mesh resolutions. Cloth vertices remaining inside the collision proxy, maximum penetration depth, and relative stretch summaries will diagnose the solver and proxy.

Stretch goals: what we hope to deliver

  1. Pose presets: A-pose, arms up, and hands on hips using skeletal skinning, with the cloth re-settling on the new pose.
  2. Lighting presets: daylight, sunset, office, and club lighting using environment maps and shadows, so users can see an outfit in different settings.
  3. Fabric presets: cotton, silk, and denim with different stiffness, weight, damping, and appearance.

GPU acceleration, photorealistic rendering, sleeves and pants, body scanning, and ML training are outside our plans.

Resources

We propose C++ with an OpenGL/GLSL viewer and a Dear ImGui interface, drawing on course cloth concepts. The demo will be a desktop program; this site is plain HTML and CSS. We expect to use team laptops and standard graphics hardware; the benchmark machine is TBD. Blender may help author geometry, but its cloth solver will not substitute for our implementation. We will prefer procedural or self-authored assets.

We will document the code starting point before development and distinguish permitted use of our HW4 work from new contributions. We will not copy or publish homework starter or solution code here; the course prohibits public posting of homework code.
